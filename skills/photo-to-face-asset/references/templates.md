### 2. 视角拼接模块（Camera / View / Frame）
- **Angle A: Frontal**
```markdown
VIEW. He faces the camera straight on, head upright and square, eyes level with the lens and looking into the front glass of it. Both ears are equally visible.

CAMERA. The camera is at the height of his own eyes and dead on axis — not above, not below, not to either side. From there you can see neither the top of his head nor the underside of his jaw. The visible test: the openings of both ears read level with the outer corners of his eyes. If the ears read higher than the eyes the camera is too low; if lower, too high.

FRAME. Square. The head from crown to chin fills about four fifths of the height of the frame, with a narrow band of background above the crown; the bottom edge cuts across the upper chest below the collarbones.
```

- **Angle B: 3/4 Quarter-Turn Looking at Lens**
```markdown
VIEW. The same man, same session, same clothing, same hair, same light. He has turned his head a quarter of the way toward his own left, so the camera sees more of his right cheek. His eyes stay on the lens. The far eye is fully open and visible but foreshortened; the near ear is fully visible; the far ear has gone. The visible test: the tip of his nose stays inside the outline of the far cheek with a small gap of cheek still showing beyond it. If the nose tip crosses that outline, the head has turned too far; if the far ear is still showing, not far enough.

CAMERA. The camera has not moved and has not tilted — it is still at the height of his own eyes and dead level. Only his head has turned. The lens sees the ridge of the near cheekbone as the most forward part of the face, and the far cheekbone as a soft raised edge against the background.

FRAME. Square. The head from crown to chin fills about four fifths of the height of the frame, the same size as in the frontal view, with slightly more background on the side his face is turned toward; the bottom edge cuts across the upper chest below the collarbones.
```

- **Angle C: 90° Profile**
```markdown
VIEW. The same man, same session, same clothing, same hair, same light. He has turned his head a full quarter turn to his own left, so one side of the head faces the camera squarely and he looks straight ahead, not at the lens. Only one eye is visible. The whole ear is visible, clear of the hair, with the front rim, the fold inside it and the lobe all readable. The visible test: the far eyebrow and the far eyelashes have disappeared completely behind the bridge of the nose. If any part of them shows, the head has not turned far enough. The profile reads as one continuous line from the forehead, over the brow, down the straight narrow bridge of the nose, past the slightly downturned tip, in to the philtrum, over both lips, in under the lower lip and out to the chin, then back along the jaw to the ear.

CAMERA. The camera has not moved and has not tilted — still at the height of his own eyes and dead level. Only his head has turned. The back of the head, the nape and the line where the hair ends at the neck are all visible.

FRAME. Square. The head from crown to chin fills about four fifths of the height of the frame, the same size as in the other two views, with more background in front of his face than behind his head; the bottom edge cuts across the upper chest below the collarbones.
```
